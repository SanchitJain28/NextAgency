## What a store migration actually involves

Moving an online store isn't like copying files between folders on a computer. Every eCommerce platform organizes its database differently. WooCommerce saves extra product details in a table called `wp_postmeta`. Magento distributes single attributes across dozens of interconnected SQL tables. Shopify uses its own structured format built around variants, collections, and Metafields.

When you switch platforms, every piece of information has to be read from the old database, cleaned up, translated into Shopify's format, and checked against the original records.

A complete migration covers five main areas:

**Your product catalog.** That includes titles, descriptions, pricing, inventory numbers, SKUs, weight, tags, product options, and every product photo.

**Your customer accounts.** Names, phone numbers, email addresses, and saved shipping or billing addresses all move over.

**Your past order history.** Every past purchase, line item, payment total, and fulfillment status moves across so customers can still see what they ordered, and your lifetime sales numbers remain accurate.

**Your categories and navigation.** Category trees, main menus, footer links, and filters get rebuilt so repeat shoppers still know where to find their usual items.

**Your written content.** Blog articles, author names, static policy pages, and FAQs move into Shopify's blog and pages setup.

Nothing gets left behind or rebuilt by guessing. We check total counts on both ends until the numbers match exactly.

## Protecting your search rankings and traffic

The biggest worry most store owners have when leaving an old platform is losing their Google rankings. That worry is completely justified. If someone changes their store address from `yourstore.com/shop/mens/shoes/leather-boot/` to `yourstore.com/products/leather-boot` without telling Google, every link pointing to that product breaks. Google treats the old page as dead, drops the ranking, and visitors see a 404 error page.

We protect your rankings through a strict technical process called 1:1 redirect mapping.

Before anything changes on your live store, we crawl every single URL on your website. That includes products, category pages, blog posts, and informational pages. We also export the list of URLs that brought in real search traffic and revenue over the past two years from your Google Search Console and analytics reports.

Then, we create a direct 301 redirect map for every single link. When a customer or Google bot visits an old WooCommerce or Magento URL, your server immediately sends them to the exact new page on Shopify. Page authority, ranking weight, and backlinks transfer over directly instead of hitting dead ends.

We also carry over your exact page titles, meta descriptions, image descriptions, and heading tags on high-traffic pages. When Google revisits your site after launch, the layout is cleaner and faster, but the core keywords and signals that earned your rankings are still right where they belong.

## Platforms we migrate from

Most of our migration work comes from three specific platforms, each with its own set of challenges.

### 1. WooCommerce and WordPress to Shopify

WooCommerce works well when a store is small. But as your catalog grows past a few hundred items and you add plugins for shipping, subscriptions, reviews, and filters, things start slowing down.

A typical WordPress store runs 30 to 50 active plugins. Every time WordPress or a plugin updates, something risks breaking. Shared hosting servers struggle under traffic spikes, and slow database queries make product pages take three to five seconds to open on a phone.

When moving from WooCommerce to Shopify:

- **We clean out plugin bloat.** Features that took four separate WordPress plugins to run — like basic discount rules, related items, and image zoom — are often handled natively inside Shopify without extra code.
- **We translate custom fields.** If your theme uses Advanced Custom Fields (ACF) for technical specs, material care guides, or size charts, we map those directly into native Shopify Metafields. You edit them cleanly from the Shopify admin without touching code.
- **We fix URL structures.** WooCommerce lets you build URLs however you want, while Shopify uses fixed folders like `/products/` and `/collections/`. We map every single old link to its new path so zero backlinks break.

### 2. Magento (Adobe Commerce) to Shopify

Merchants usually leave Magento because running it has become too expensive. A mid-sized Magento store often requires dedicated cloud hosting on AWS costing $3,000 to $10,000 every month, plus costly retainers for developers just to install regular security patches.

When moving from Magento to Shopify:

- **We simplify complex catalogs.** Magento's configurable products often have dozens of attribute sets and options. We map those cleanly into Shopify product options or separate connected items, making inventory easier to manage day to day.
- **We replace expensive server setups.** You move from self-managed servers to Shopify's managed infrastructure. There are no security patches to install by hand, no server memory limits to configure, and no emergency weekend downtime when traffic spikes.
- **We rebuild custom wholesale and B2B pricing.** If you run tiered pricing, dealer discounts, or customer-specific price lists on Magento, we set those up through Shopify Plus B2B tools or private app logic.

### 3. Upgrading to Shopify 2.0 and Store Consolidations

Not every migration starts on a competitor. Many stores are already on Shopify, but their setup is five or six years old:

- **Shopify 2.0 Theme Upgrades.** Older "vintage" Shopify themes use hardcoded Liquid templates. If you want to add a banner, an FAQ section, or a video block to a product page, you have to edit theme code. Upgrading to an Online Store 2.0 theme gives you drag-and-drop sections across every page, making everyday marketing updates fast and simple.
- **Shopify-to-Shopify Consolidations.** If your business expanded by opening separate Shopify stores for different countries or sister brands, you might be paying multiple app fees and updating inventory in three places at once. We consolidate those stores into one central Shopify admin using Shopify Markets, cutting your monthly software costs and keeping your stock counts in sync.

## How we do it: The 5-phase migration process

We run every migration through five structured phases. This keeps the project on schedule and makes sure your live store stays online and selling throughout the build.

### Phase 1: Catalog Audit & Architecture Plan

We start by reviewing your current store's database. We check how many products, variants, and customer records you have, identify duplicate records or missing SKUs, and list every active plugin.

We group every feature your old store currently uses into one of three buckets:
1. Features built directly into Shopify.
2. Features handled by a well-reviewed Shopify App.
3. Custom features that need private Liquid or script logic.

We also run a full crawl of your site to record every live URL for our redirect matrix.

### Phase 2: Staging Environment Setup

We set up a private, password-protected Shopify development store. Your live store remains completely untouched on its existing server.

Next, we run a test import using a sample batch of your real catalog — usually 10% to 20% of your products, collections, and customers. This lets us verify that custom fields, variant options, prices, and images appear in the right spots before running the full import.

### Phase 3: Theme & Feature Rebuilding

With the test data in place, we set up your new theme. You can choose to replicate your existing layout or use the migration as an opportunity to build a faster, cleaner mobile experience on Online Store 2.0.

During this phase, we connect your payment gateways, set up your shipping zones and rates, configure automated email flows in tools like Klaviyo, and hook up your inventory or warehouse systems.

### Phase 4: Pre-Launch Delta Sync

A few days before launch, we test the entire store end to end. We place real test orders using credit cards, test discount codes, check shipping rates at checkout, and confirm email receipts on phones, tablets, and laptops.

Because your old store remained open during the build, customers placed new orders and created new accounts while we were working. The delta sync solves this: our automated scripts pull only the newly created orders and customer profiles that occurred since the initial import. Both databases are brought into balance.

### Phase 5: Zero-Downtime DNS Cutover

Once you give final approval, we schedule the domain switchover during your lowest-traffic window.

Two days before launch, we lower your domain's TTL (Time To Live) setting to five minutes. When launch time arrives, we point your domain's DNS records to Shopify. Because the TTL was shortened, the change updates worldwide in minutes.

Your old store turns off, your new Shopify store turns on, and customers never see an outage. We verify your SSL certificate, place a live test purchase, submit your new XML sitemap to Google Search Console, and begin our 30-day monitoring window.

## Common technical challenges and how we handle them

Moving a store comes with real technical constraints. Here is how we handle the four most common ones:

### 1. Customer passwords cannot be exported

Every modern eCommerce platform protects customer passwords using one-way encryption hashing (like bcrypt or SHA-256). That means your old database does not actually know what a customer's password is — it only knows the scrambled hash. It is mathematically impossible to turn those hashes back into plain text, and Shopify's security rules will not let anyone inject third-party password hashes directly.

**Our fix:** We migrate all customer contact details, addresses, and purchase histories. After launch, we trigger branded account activation emails. When a customer clicks the link in their email, they enter a new password and immediately see their past orders waiting for them.

### 2. Custom order statuses from WooCommerce or Magento

WooCommerce and Magento let merchants create custom order status labels like "Awaiting Custom Fabrication" or "Sent to Embossing." Shopify uses fixed order states: paid or unpaid, fulfilled or unfulfilled.

**Our fix:** We translate your historical orders into Shopify's standard fulfillment states, and attach the exact legacy status notes into order tags and custom Metafields. Your customer service team can search and read those historical notes directly inside the Shopify admin.

### 3. Products with dozens of options

Standard Shopify stores historically allowed up to 100 variants and three option dropdowns per product. If you sell a shirt with five sizes, four colors, and three sleeve lengths, that equals 60 variants — well within the limit. But if you sell custom machinery or furniture with thousands of combinations, you hit a ceiling.

**Our fix:** We structure complex products cleanly. We can link parent and child products using visual swatches, connect options through the newer Shopify 2,000-variant APIs, or use custom line-item properties for personalized text or custom measurements.

### 4. Custom product specifications

Many WooCommerce and Magento stores save extra specifications — like downloadable PDF user manuals, fabric care instructions, or B2B minimum order quantities — in custom database tables.

**Our fix:** We map each custom data field into a native Shopify Metafield. When you add or edit a product in Shopify, those fields appear right on the product editing screen.

## Why work with an independent developer instead of an agency?

Large agencies often pitch migrations with a team of four or five people: an account director, a project manager, a junior designer, and an offshore developer.

That setup introduces three real problems for store owners:

1. **Information gets lost in transit.** You explain how your inventory rules work to an account manager, who writes it in an internal ticket for a developer they have never met. Nuances get lost, edge cases get missed, and bugs show up right before launch.
2. **You pay for agency overhead.** A significant portion of your project fee pays for agency management, sales commissions, and internal meetings rather than actual engineering work.
3. **Turnaround times drag out.** Simple adjustments take days to move through agency approval queues.

When you work with an independent senior developer, you communicate directly with the person writing your database scripts, building your theme sections, and verifying your 301 redirects. Questions get answered immediately, edge cases get caught during code review, and projects finish on fixed milestone schedules.

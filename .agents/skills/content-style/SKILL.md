# Clean, Plain English Content Style Guide

This skill defines a voice, vocabulary, and writing style for any kind of writing. It ensures a piece reads cleanly, naturally, and convincingly in everyday English without inflated buzzwords or overly complex phrasing. It is domain-agnostic: apply it to a product page, an internal memo, a help article, a pitch, a research summary, or a plain email.

---

## 1. Core Writing Principles

1. **Speak like a practical expert**: Explain things the way someone knowledgeable explains them to a smart non-expert, in plain conversation.
2. **Clarity beats complexity**: If a sentence sounds complicated, break it down. If a word sounds pretentious, replace it with an everyday word.
3. **Concrete over abstract**: Never make a vague claim when you can describe a simple, real scenario instead.
4. **Short, direct sentences**: State the fact clearly, give the practical context, and move on.
5. **Match the reader**: Write for whoever will actually read this — a customer, a teammate, a beginner, an executive — and use words they'd use.

---

## 2. Jargon Blacklist & Plain English Replacements

Avoid inflated vocabulary, pretentious descriptors, and vague buzzwords. These words are banned because they're harder to understand — they exist to make writing sound impressive, not to communicate.

| Don't use                       | Use instead                                              |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bespoke                         | Custom                                                   |
| Seamlessly integrates           | Connects to / works with                                 |
| Robust architecture             | Built to handle [specific thing]                         |
| Unlock your potential           | (state the actual outcome)                               |
| Holistic transformation         | (name the specific change)                               |
| Operational model               | How it works day to day                                  |
| Leverage                        | Use                                                      |
| Cutting-edge / state-of-the-art | (name the specific technology or method)                 |
| Ecosystem                       | (name the actual tools/systems)                          |
| Scalable                        | Handles more [users/orders/data] without breaking        |
| Streamline                      | Remove steps / make faster                               |
| Empower                         | Let you / give you the ability to                        |
| Best-in-class                   | (state the actual result or comparison)                  |
| Synergy                         | (delete — rarely means anything)                         |
| Solution                        | (name the actual thing — product, tool, method, service) |
| Utilize                         | Use                                                      |
| Facilitate                      | Help / make easier                                       |
| In order to                     | To                                                       |
| Deliver value / add value       | (state what the reader actually gets)                    |

If a word could be dropped into any other document, on any topic, with zero changes, it's too vague. Replace it with the specific thing being described or the specific result the reader will see.

## 3. Layout & Structure Rules

### A. Format Flow: Heading → Direct Content

- Start with a clear, descriptive heading.
- Follow immediately with readable, high-density paragraphs.
- Avoid unnecessary boxed cards or artificial number badges (`01`, `02`, `03`) unless they label genuinely distinct items.

### B. Use Relatable, Concrete Examples

Ground every claim or point in a specific, everyday situation the reader would recognize. Don't describe an abstract concept — describe what a real person would actually see, do, or experience.

#### Examples (Bad vs Good):

- ❌ "This delivers a bespoke solution that unlocks your true potential."
- ✅ "You set up the exact steps you need, instead of forcing your work into a fixed template."

- ❌ "A holistic approach that seamlessly integrates cutting-edge methods into your operational model."
- ✅ "The two tools you already use now pass information back and forth on their own."

- ❌ "Leveraging modular abstractions for dynamic cross-functional optimization."
- ✅ "Change one setting and everything that depends on it updates too — no re-doing it by hand."

- ❌ "Users gain autonomous content sovereignty through native extensibility."
- ✅ "You can edit the wording yourself afterward, without asking anyone for help."

### C. Honest Tradeoffs Build Trust

- Never oversell a complex approach.
- When explaining a harder, more custom, or more expensive option, state honestly what it costs in time, money, or upkeep. Say clearly when a simpler alternative gets the same result for less.

### D. Visual & Image Layout (when the format includes images)

_Applies only when the output is a visual layout — a web page, slide deck, one-pager, or similar. Skip for plain text._

- Don't insert massive full-width image blocks that fill the whole screen or page.
- On wider layouts, alternate a two-column pattern:
  - **Section A**: Text on the left, supporting image on the right.
  - **Section B**: Supporting image on the left, text on the right.
- Keep images styled with clean, consistent borders and a short caption underneath.

## 4. Headlines, Titles & Calls to Action

- Headlines and titles state a concrete outcome or fact, not a mood ("Loads in under 2 seconds," not "Blazing-fast performance"; "How to reset your password in 3 steps," not "Take control of your account").
- Where the writing asks the reader to do something, name the action and, where possible, the result ("See a sample," not "Begin your journey"; "Download the checklist," not "Get started today").
- Never use exclamation points to manufacture excitement.

---

## 5. FAQ & Q-and-A Writing Rules

_Applies whenever you're writing question-and-answer content: FAQs, help articles, support replies._

- **Answer length**: Keep every answer concise and direct — aim for **30 to 35 words**. Adjust the target if a given format calls for shorter or longer, but never pad.
- **No preamble**: Answer in the very first sentence. Avoid filler like _"That's a great question…"_ or _"When considering this…"_.
- **Plain English**: Use short words and clear outcomes.

### Good Example:

> **Question:** Will this add extra steps to my process?
> **Answer:** No. It replaces several manual steps with one automatic one. Most people report the whole task now takes under a minute, down from ten or more before. _(28 words)_

---

## 6. Pre-Publication Checklist

Before finalizing any copy, verify:

1. [ ] **No blacklisted words**: Check every word against the Section 2 table. Replace anything vague with the specific thing or outcome.
2. [ ] **Concrete examples**: Are examples easy to picture, not abstract?
3. [ ] **Headings → content**: Is the layout a clean reading flow without unnecessary cards?
4. [ ] **Headlines & actions**: Do they state a fact or action, not a feeling?
5. [ ] **Q-and-A length**: Are answers concise (around 30–35 words) and preamble-free?
6. [ ] **Read-aloud test**: Does it sound like a knowledgeable human talking, or like AI/marketing fluff?

## 7. Examples: Bad vs Good (across contexts)

**Report / summary**

- ❌ "The initiative leverages synergies to drive holistic outcomes."
- ✅ "Two teams that used to do the same work separately now share one checklist, saving about a day each week."

**Help doc / instructions**

- ❌ "Utilize the robust configuration interface to streamline your setup."
- ✅ "Open Settings and turn on 'Auto-save.' That's the whole setup."

**Announcement / email**

- ❌ "We're excited to empower users with best-in-class capabilities!"
- ✅ "You can now undo a sent message for up to 30 seconds after sending it."

**Product or service description**

- ❌ "State-of-the-art materials ensure an elevated experience."
- ✅ "The handle stays cool because it's wrapped in silicone, so you can lift the pot straight off the stove."

**General performance claim**

- ❌ "Our scalable architecture ensures seamless performance under load."
- ✅ "It kept running normally when traffic jumped 10x overnight — we tested it before launch."

**Persuasive / marketing line**

- ❌ "Dynamic personalization touchpoints drive engagement across the journey."
- ✅ "Returning visitors see the items they saved last time, instead of the same banner everyone gets."
